Imagine walking out of your old, damp apartment room, stepping directly sideways into a perfectly insulated, brand-new modular home, and watching your monthly electricity and water bills drop close to zero.

That’s the vision behind The Cushion — a proposed decentralized infrastructure project designed to automate human survival and free daily life from the constant race to outrun inflation, rent, and crumbling infrastructure. It isn’t built yet, and no city has deployed it. What follows is a concrete design proposal, not a description of something already running.

**The Problem with Old Housing**

Many neighborhoods are genuinely trapped this way. Aging concrete walk-up apartments (gong-yu) commonly trap humidity, develop mold, and carry real earthquake risk. Traditional urban renewal (du-geng) often fails because developers and residents end up in decades-long legal disputes over square footage, while the buildings continue to decay in the meantime. This is a real, well-documented problem, not an invented one.

The proposal is to route around that gridlock: a software-based tax, embedded in the infrastructure layer of automated commercial systems — what this archive elsewhere calls LICT — would capture a share of the value generated by AI and automation companies and direct it toward manufacturing modular housing units. This is a design proposal with no current legal or technical basis in any jurisdiction, not an existing or operating funding mechanism.

**The Proposed Layout**

These proposed modules — called AVA (Autonomous Voxel Architecture) — are envisioned as lightweight, interlocking units, conceptually similar to large, engineered building blocks. Specific materials (carbon fiber composites, aerogel insulation) and specific mechanisms (an external hoist system, magnetic-levitation elevators, atmospheric water condensation, waste-to-fertilizer processing) are proposed directions worth real engineering development — not technologies that currently exist in deployable, tested form at this scale. Each one is a genuine area of existing research (atmospheric water generation and maglev transit both exist in other contexts), but combining them into an affordable, mass-produced housing retrofit is a substantial unsolved engineering and manufacturing challenge, not a solved one.

The general concept — a lightweight exterior addition that doesn’t require demolishing or structurally altering an existing building — is a reasonable design direction worth pursuing, especially for buildings where demolition is contentious or unaffordable. Whether the specific mechanisms described above are the right ones, or the only ones, is an open engineering question.

**Securing a Livelihood**

The practical question any proposal like this has to answer: if housing and utilities are covered, how does someone afford groceries and daily needs?

The proposed answer connects to an idea developed elsewhere in this archive: as automated systems handle more manufacturing and manual labor, considered human attention and evaluation become more economically significant, not less. There’s a real, if disputed, concern in AI research that systems trained increasingly on their own synthetic output can degrade in quality over time — a documented phenomenon called model collapse, though how severe or imminent a risk it poses to today’s frontier systems remains a matter of real scientific disagreement, not a settled crisis. If that concern has merit, it strengthens the case for valuing human-generated content and evaluation — though the proposal doesn’t depend on this specific risk being proven to make its broader economic case.

Under this proposal, people would be compensated — through the same proposed LICT mechanism — for activities currently uncompensated by any market: staying healthy, maintaining daily mobility, recording local history, caring for neighbors. Whether automation companies would actually be willing, or could be required, to fund this kind of compensation is a real open question this piece doesn’t resolve — it’s a proposed design, not a guaranteed or inevitable outcome.
