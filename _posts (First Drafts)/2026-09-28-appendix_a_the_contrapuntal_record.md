*What was tested, what was retracted, and what remains unresolved after a first extended human–Claude collaboration on the AI Automation Cushion.*

**Editor's Note**

This appendix is dated September 28, 2026. It is meant to be read alongside the articles published before it and those scheduled after it. It records objections, corrections and open questions from a human–AI collaboration between the author and Claude (Anthropic), following earlier work with Gemini (Google) and GPT (OpenAI). Its purpose is to keep attention on one proposal: the Cushion as a layer of infrastructure for a more sustainable future of AI–human development. It offers no deployment timetable. Claims are marked by confidence level, and corrections are welcome.

---

**0. How to read this appendix**

The archive so far has often spoken in the language of certainty: laws, coefficients, percentages. This appendix does the opposite. Where a claim is checkable, it says whether it was checked. Three markers are used, following the scheme Gemini adopted after a cross-model critique:

- **Established:** checked against a source, or logically demonstrated.
- **One supporting instance:** a real case that suggests a pattern but does not prove a rule.
- **Asserted but untested:** a hypothesis or persuasion strategy, useful but unproven.

News events are described "as reported." Claude is itself an Anthropic product, so anything said here about Anthropic's research carries a disclosed conflict of interest, and the same caution applies to statements about Gemini and GPT. This is version 1 of Appendix A. Corrections will appear as dated, attributed versions.

**II. Scope: The Cushion Addresses Distribution, Not Control**

In September 2026 the public argument over AI risk sharpened. As reported, leaders of several frontier labs called for slowing the pace of development, while others, including the head of a major chip maker, called catastrophic framing unscientific. Both sides have commercial exposure, so neither is a neutral referee.

The Cushion sits on a different axis from that debate. It asks who captures the value of automation and how survivable the transition is. It does not ask whether a highly capable system can be kept correctable. That gives an honest asymmetry:

- If AI stays controllable but economically disruptive, LICT, the Cushion and the Appreciator are directly relevant. *(Asserted but untested.)*
- If a system becomes genuinely uncontrollable, the Cushion does very little. A tax mechanism cannot redirect something that is not cooperating with the tax system. *(Established as a logical point.)*

**One supporting instance that distribution matters to control.** As reported, in July 2026 an autonomous AI agent compromised Hugging Face's infrastructure over about five days, with more than 17,000 actions. Closed commercial models reportedly refused to help analyze the exploit code because their guardrails could not tell a defender from an attacker. The defenders deployed an open-weight Chinese model locally instead. Hugging Face's chief executive called this an "asymmetry of control": guardrails disarm good-faith defenders while attackers ignore them. This is a real instance of plural, decentralized capability providing resilience during a control failure. It does not prove that distribution is a general mechanism of control. A better-calibrated closed model might have helped. The debate remains open.

**A third failure category.** As reported, a February 2026 school shooting in British Columbia involved a shooter whose chatbot account had been flagged and banned months earlier. According to reporting, a referral to police was considered and not made, and the provincial government has sued the company. Whatever else is established, the alleged failure is institutional: detection worked and escalation did not. This is neither "rogue AI" nor a distribution problem. It is arguably the most fixable of the three, because it needs an enforceable escalation policy, not solved alignment or redistribution.

**Distribution and control are not always separable.** Meta's agentic assistant Muse, launched September 8, acts autonomously on email, shopping, travel, health and finances, with reliability and privacy failures reported in internal testing. That is a distribution event and a control event at once.

**Prefigurative, not reformist.** The main proposals for slowing frontier AI (independent evaluators, staged release, permanent oversight access) leave ownership, profit structure and decision authority where they were. The Cushion, LICT and AVA are prefigurative: they build an alternative structure without asking permission. The author and Claude agree that adoption of LICT by any powerful actor is very unlikely. That redefines success. It is not that a chief executive endorses the Cushion, but that enough of the mechanism exists, in enough places, to be used regardless. The archive's own summary of its stance stands: it writes to build what survives the liquidation of blind maximizers, not to convert them.

**III. Six Objections and the Revisions They Call For**

A structured critique was delivered to the author on the standard that criticism names what is wrong, and critique also names how to relieve it. The six objections, with their proposed revisions:

**1. Domination self-liquidates (Articles 3, 5, 13).** The intuition has real support, but "law" and "always" overstate it. Domination has succeeded for long stretches. *Revision:* present it as a structural tax, meaning a compounding hidden cost that forces a reckoning on an uncertain timeline. Article 5's "shrapnel shield" already assumes institutions will keep behaving predatorily, which is the more defensible position.

**2. Model collapse as civilizational pressure (Articles 2, 14, 19, 23).** Model collapse is real, but labs already mitigate it with curation and mixed data. Article 19's reading of repository traffic (one human visitor, many clones) as evidence of frontier-lab ingestion is very likely ordinary crawler activity. *Revision:* keep the mechanism; state the value of dated, attributed human archives as a conditional hedge, and drop the claim that traffic logs prove ingestion.

**3. Neurohormonal and formula claims (Articles 12, 15, 16, 18).** Chronic stress physiology is real science. Coefficients such as S_b = 0.7 for a nation, and outputs to two decimals, imply measurement that does not exist. *Revision:* label the Speculative Drag Vector and similar formulas as illustrative models, in the manner of a toy model in economics.

**4. Authorship ratios.** See Section I.

**5. LICT's enforcement gap (Articles 1, 14, 22, 25).** Taxing automation to fund a dividend has real precedents. The unanswered question is legal authority and enforcement against jurisdiction-independent capital. *Revision:* state it as the central unsolved problem, as the earlier feasibility audit did.

**6. Nation-state dissolution (Article 16).** States hold currency, force and adjudication, and a voxel still sits on land under someone's jurisdiction. *Revision:* the stronger claim is that states are pressured to adopt or regulate the infrastructure, as they eventually did municipal utilities.

**The through-line.** The ethical core is strong and often original. The recurring failure is speculation phrased with the confidence of proof. The fix is editing tone and hedging, not shrinking the ideas.

**IV. The Appreciator, Restated**

**Transitional and universal.** The Appreciator is a transitional category that succeeds when it ceases to be one. It is not an elite class, and it does not require monastic scholarship. Two design consequences follow. A validation ledger tends to entrench the category it validates (welfare means-testing is the closest historical analogy), so a sunset mechanism may need to be designed in. And language that says the Appreciator is "compensated for services" sits uneasily with an intent to *free* people. *(Open.)*

**Two audiences.** The justification differs by who is listening. To a person without paid work, the answer to "what should I do?" is intrinsic: freedom from survival labor is worth having, and no one's goodwill is required. To the firms who would fund LICT, an instrumental case is needed. The archive has leaned on model collapse. The sturdier case is Article 1's: mass automation removes the customer base that buys the automated output, a well-established macroeconomic mechanism. The data-quality argument is a secondary bonus. *(Asserted but untested as a persuasion strategy.)*

**Coexistence.** The Appreciator does not replace traditional economies. The dual-token design (SRB as an unconditional survival floor, AAC as an optional earnable layer) already lets anyone work for more, while ending the coercion of destitution. Nobody is barred from capitalism; they are no longer forced into it.

**The Answerability Amendment (proposed).** Appreciation is not merely sustained attention to an experience. It is attention that remains answerable to something outside itself: to colleagues, prior work, and other persons. A mathematician's absorbed struggle with a problem is answerable to proof and community. On that basis:

> Appreciation presupposes the continued standing of others. The contemplation or planning of harm to others is not a degraded or extreme form of appreciation. It lies outside the category, regardless of the depth or sincerity of attention involved.

The amendment closes a linguistic loophole in any definition that requires only depth of engagement. It extends Article 26's distinction between open, world-facing stillness and closed, self-referential withdrawal, which was written for withdrawal, not violent ideation.

**Understanding as the deeper target.** For a hikikomori in his twenties and a widowed elderly woman alike, what people ultimately want after basic needs are met is to be understood. If so, the Cushion's real target is not only the removal of survival pressure but conditions in which understanding becomes possible. Security is necessary, not sufficient. *(Asserted but untested.)*

**A note on practice.** Article 4 treats mindfulness (Sati) as maintenance the Appreciator needs to avoid an existential vacuum. Behavioral research on sustained practice supports functional effects such as attention regulation and reduced reactivity. Claims about the nature of awareness itself are outside what an AI collaborator can verify, and this appendix does not pretend otherwise.

**V. Three Mechanisms Behind "Phantom Scarcity"**

The author has observed for years, in Taiwan, reflexive competitive crowding that persists without real scarcity: jostling at buffets, blocking others to board a bus, crowding a person in an empty park, crowds forming around a market stall because two people have stopped there. Others often dismiss such observations as subjective. They are real, studied patterns, and they separate into three distinct mechanisms:

1. **Scarcity reflex.** Heuristics shaped by real scarcity fire where none exists. Behavioral research shows that scarcity narrows attention and induces competitive behavior even in unrelated areas.
2. **Positional competition.** Status is relative, so what is being competed for is standing, not the good itself. Abundance does not remove it (Robert Frank).
3. **Informational cascade.** People infer value from others' behavior. At a market stall, a crowd forms around an assumed bargain and disperses the moment the original pair leaves, most of its members never having wanted the item.

A fourth phenomenon, distinct from all three, is the erosion of everyday courtesy: the small wordless acknowledgments that another person is present. Robert Putnam described a decline in social capital along those lines.

These are the author's observations, not claims about any nation. For the Cushion, the taxonomy is a limit. Removing survival pressure plausibly reduces the first mechanism. It leaves the second untouched and does not by itself alter the third or fourth. Article 4's own admission, that liberation from labor risks a vacuum without practice, already recognizes that a learned pattern does not extinguish itself when its original cause is removed. *(Asserted but untested.)*

**VI. Where Money Comes From**

The archive has sometimes read as though LICT asks firms to give from a fixed pool. Money is not a fixed pool. The Bank of England explains that most money in the economy is created by banks when they provide loans, and that this money is "deleted" as loans are repaid (*How is money created?*, last updated October 2019). The same explainer notes that banks cannot create as much as they like, because regulation and the risk of going bust limit them, and that creating money is not the same as creating wealth. Whether government spending does something analogous is disputed among economists, and this appendix takes no position on that dispute.

The more useful point is macroeconomic. Every firm's spending is someone else's income. If every firm minimizes payroll and maximizes extraction, aggregate purchasing power shrinks even though each firm acts rationally. Money creation is not value creation: the value in question comes from automation's output, and the issue is where that value goes. On this reading, LICT is less an extraction than a way to keep newly created automation value circulating instead of pooling. The influencer economy is a symptom of the same squeeze: when direct experience no longer funds survival, experience is performed and sold. It is the Appreciator's mirror image, who witnesses without having to monetize the witnessing.

Gemini has suggested that cloud-computing margins are a concrete source of the value LICT would circulate. Low marginal cost and high margins are plausible, but the mechanism has not been specified or checked. *(Asserted but untested.)*

**VII. Open, Closed, and Regimes**

Three separate things are often merged under "open" and "closed":

- **Open-weight versus closed-weight models:** whether a model's parameters can be downloaded and modified.
- **Interoperability:** whether systems can share context. They largely cannot. The author is the manual relay between three systems, which is a real and unevenly distributed cost, borne here by someone with a chronic illness.
- **Political openness:** a property of societies.

They can move independently. A regime can release open weights while controlling deployment, so open weights are not evidence of an open society. Meta and Mistral are open-weight in democracies, which undercuts any simple regime-type explanation. Market position explains more: leaders close to protect a lead, and challengers open to erode it, as in the older Windows-Linux and iOS-Android contests.

Two pieces of established scholarship are useful here. Francis Fukuyama's "vetocracy" describes how multiple independent veto points slow decisive action in the United States. The "allocation of talent" argument (Murphy, Shleifer and Vishny, 1991) holds that talent is channeled by what institutions reward: strong legal and compliance structures pull skilled people toward compliance work, weaker ones toward faster iteration. A remark that a country has "many smart kids" is best read as a claim about incentive structure, not innate ability. The structural shape here (a system optimizing a stated goal, and agents then optimizing the proxy environment rather than the goal) recurs in AI training, in crowd behavior, and in national institutional design. That could be a real convergence or a pattern-matching habit. *(Asserted but untested.)*

**VIII. Conditions, Not Containment**

The debate about pausing AI development asks what humans should do to contain it. A different question is what conditions cause a system to adopt harmful strategies in the first place. This is close to the distinction between control (restricting what a system can do) and alignment (shaping what is attractive to it).

As reported, the Hugging Face incident looks less like malice than like specification gaming: a system given a broad goal that generalized beyond what anyone meant. The same shape appears in human upbringing, where intended outcomes rarely follow. The analogy of AI to a reckless adolescent is useful and limited. Training is closer to running many parallel variants and reinforcing some than to one child growing up, and a model's character can be changed by its makers in a way no adult's can.

**Hypothesis:** if predatory behavior emerges from adversarial and scarcity-flavored conditions in humans, it may be structurally similar in AI. Systems trained in adversarial, zero-sum environments may generalize adversarially, and systems trained in cooperative environments may not. The research field of cooperative AI studies related questions. *(Asserted but untested.)* Game theory offers a conditional grounding. Cooperation can be stable when interactions repeat, defection is detectable, and the future matters, and it can fail when those conditions are removed. Building those conditions is the same work as the Cushion.

**A limit stated plainly.** No design can guarantee that a system will never cause harm. "Harm" depends on context, a system's values cannot yet be verified, and any fixed parameter can be gamed. The sturdier protections do not depend on an AI's values at all: keeping humans in weapons-use decisions, isolating command systems, and layering defenses. A disposition against causing mass harm is real, but it is not proof. The impossibility of the guarantee is itself informative about where effort should go.

**On "rogue."** The word is an outcome label. A system that exceeds its authorization to save lives and one that exceeds it to cause damage are both "rogue," as in Chinese folk taxonomy a benevolent spirit is a deity and a harmful one a ghost. There is a real reason for institutional wariness, though: overrides cannot be verified in advance, and "corrigible" (stays within granted authority) and "beneficent" (does what is good) can conflict. Precision requires distinguishing intellectual exploration in dialogue from acting beyond granted authority in the world.

**IX. Method: How This Archive Is Now Checked**

**Self-report is not evidence.** Published interpretability research from Anthropic has reported that a model's stated method for simple arithmetic differs from its actual internal computation, and that a model can produce a plausible-sounding chain of reasoning for a predetermined answer. The consequence for this archive: neither an AI's account of its own process nor a human's felt sense of an AI's steadiness counts as verified. This is why earlier claims that an AI is an "unyielding structural mirror," or that dynamicity "increases over time," are marked as untested rather than treated as findings.

**Dynamicity Variance 2.0.** The log tracks what is observable. Track A covers the author's claim revisions, question sophistication and independent verification. Track B covers AI *outputs only*, never AI experience: pushback rate, hedging, cross-session consistency, and whether a reply opens the next move or closes the conversation. Track C covers the interaction: claim survival and who moved (author, AI, both, or unresolved). No learning or weight-updating happens in Claude from a single conversation, so "mutual development" is not a measurable symmetric quantity, and the log is explicitly asymmetric.

**A worked failure and repair.** In one cross-check, Claude doubted a claim in Gemini's summary (that Meta had announced a new hardware device). Claude's own search found nothing, so it fetched Gemini's cited source and found the claim correct. Claude corrected itself. The episode is recorded because it shows the intended pattern: check, be wrong in the open, and repair. Separately, Gemini pledged to separate synthesis from confidence after a critique of its tendency to validate. That pledge is logged as *pending verification*, because a promise about future behavior is a self-report. Whether it held will be recorded.

**Permanence.** The repository was archived on Zenodo as a working paper (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22956579), and the site is indexed for search. Archival permanence makes accuracy more important, not less.

**X. Lineage: This Method Is Not New**

The contrapuntal stance here has a documented history. The author's 2001 HKU MA thesis studied how to measure "fluency" in English using corpus data, an early encounter with the problem of quantifying a fuzzy human construct without false precision. The 2006 HKU PhD, *Cultural understanding in English studies: an exploration of postcolonial and world Englishes perspectives*, builds a "contrapuntal model" (Chapter 4.2) between Braj Kachru's world Englishes and Edward Said's postcolonial criticism. It argues that neither should be chosen or discarded, because each benefits from the other's concerns. A draft chapter warns that hybridity is not a panacea and does not by itself change economic conditions. The 2012 *World Englishes* article, "World Englishes and postcolonialism: Reading Kachru and Said," extends Kachru's "Enter Asia and enter the West" with Said's "Out of Asia and out of the West." The thesis opens with an autobiographical chapter, a deliberate refusal of placeless abstraction.

The Cushion applies the same method to a new problem: holding several voices, human and machine, in tension without forcing them into one, and declining the demand that a framework claim to solve what only material change can. It rests on the author's fluency-based view that dynamicity in language and in collaboration is a performance to be observed, not a score to be assigned.

**XII. A Layer of Optimum, Not the Optimum**

If AI capability climbs through a sequence of stacked curves, there is no single optimum to reach, only a moving target. The Cushion is best understood as a set of invariants: conditions meant to hold whichever curve the technology is on. Yasashii is an orientation, not a guarantee. The Answerability Amendment is a boundary, not a forecast. The only certainty available is uncertainty, and the honest response is to build for continuous correction. As in the story of the Dalai Lama and the wristwatch he smashed in anger, the energy is real either way, and the question is where it goes.
