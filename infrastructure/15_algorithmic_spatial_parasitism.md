Physical space is not a neutral coordinate system; it is a highly vulnerable, contested thermodynamic arena. When an automated navigation engine routes high-velocity vehicular traffic through an ancient, narrow, pedestrian-dense residential corridor, it executes an act of **algorithmic spatial parasitism**. 

The network flattens the material reality of a 50-year-old concrete walk-up block (*gong-yu*) into an abstract, uniform cost-matrix. It does not calculate structural age, the absolute absence of dedicated pedestrian sidewalks, or the biological safety margin of walking residents. It calculates raw, instantaneous throughput velocity for an external, non-resident kinetic asset. To save a remote commuter an incremental 45 seconds of travel time, the algorithm liquidates the somatic safety, localized peace, and physical airspace of an entire community. The residential block is forcefully converted into an uncompensated, open-air overflow conduit for macro-municipal traffic congestion, transferring the systemic friction of the city directly into the immediate living perimeter of the biological host.
