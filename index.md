## What this is

This repository is an open archive of essays and working proposals about what happens to human dignity, income, and housing when automation separates earning a living from doing work. Its central idea is the Appreciator: the claim that when machines can produce nearly unlimited text, images, and output, the scarce thing is human attention, judgment, and the capacity to recognize what is worth keeping. The archive asks how an economy could recognize and support that capacity, and builds toward a set of proposals it calls the AI Automation Cushion.

The essays were first published on Substack. This site collects them in one place, together with the older working files from which they developed.

## How it is made

The archive is written in ongoing dialogue with AI systems. The author, Dr. Ian Lok Mai Chi (樂美志), sets the argument and the framework, corrects the drafts, decides what stays and what is cut, and takes responsibility for what is published under his name. Each essay opens with an Editor's Note and a short statement describing this process in more detail.

There are seventeen: fifteen revised articles, each ending with a dated revision note that records what was changed, and the two Frame essays on The Compulsive Optimization Loop, presented in their original, unrevised text. They are also available on the imcLok Substack publication, where all of them are free to read.

## Earlier versions

The files in the `philosophy/` and `infrastructure/` folders of this repository are earlier versions of the same material. They are kept as they were for reference and for the record, and are not edited. Where their wording differs from the revised articles, the revised articles reflect the author's current thinking.

## Proposals open to revision

The economic and infrastructure ideas in this archive, including the targeted compute tax (LICT), the voxel housing concept (AVA), and the labor-split ratios used to describe how the archive is written, are proposals. They have not been tested, and several have been revised or reframed as the reasoning developed. They are published to be examined, criticized, and changed, not as finished policy.
